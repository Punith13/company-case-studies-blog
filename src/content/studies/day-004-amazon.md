When people say “Amazon,” they may picture a parcel arriving at their door. But the company also sells cloud computing, advertising, subscriptions and services to independent merchants. Each business has **different costs, margins and capital needs**.

Amazon is a useful case because it answers a counterintuitive question: **How can a company become more profitable while its free cash flow turns negative?**

> **The big idea:** A profitable business can spend more cash on future capacity than it brings in from current operations. That is not the same thing as posting an operating loss.

## Three businesses under one financial roof

Amazon officially reports three operating segments:

**North America** includes much of its regional online and physical retail activity. **International** covers stores and services outside North America. **AWS** sells cloud infrastructure and related services to organisations.

Advertising, subscriptions and third-party seller services are important revenue streams, but they are **not** separate operating segments in the segment-profit table.

The same retail customer may buy goods, pay for Prime and view sponsored products. Meanwhile a software company may pay AWS for servers, storage and compute. The payments have very different economics.

## The income statement: AWS is the profit engine

These are **US$ billions for Q3**, rounded.

| Segment | Q3 2025 sales | Q3 2026 sales | Q3 2026 operating income | Q3 2026 margin |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| North America | 106.27 | 112.85 | 8.72 | 7.7% |
| International | 40.90 | 43.11 | 1.58 | 3.7% |
| AWS | 33.01 | 45.98 | 17.77 | 38.7% |
| **Total** | **180.17** | **201.94** | **28.08** | **13.9%** |

Source: [Amazon Q3 2026 earnings release](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/), quarter ended 30 September 2026.

AWS contributed only about **22.8% of Amazon's Q3 sales** but approximately **63.3% of operating income**. Retail is enormous, yet cloud computing produced most of the operating profit that quarter.

AWS operating margin of **38.7%** compares with **7.7%** in North America. That is why looking only at Amazon's group revenue conceals the story. The amount sold matters, but **how much is kept from each dollar sold** matters just as much.

There is a second twist. Amazon's consolidated operating income increased from **$17.42 billion to $28.08 billion** between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026, but reported net income edged down from **$21.19 billion to $20.69 billion**. Non-operating items, including gains in the earlier period, help explain why net income did not follow operating income in a straight line. Profit headlines need context.

## The balance sheet: a business building its future

| Balance sheet, US$ billions | Dec 2025 | Sep 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 86.81 | 55.78 |
| Inventory | 38.33 | 42.63 |
| Accounts payable | 121.91 | 158.72 |
| Property and equipment, net | 357.03 | 508.86 |
| Long-term debt | 65.65 | 173.13 |

Source: [Amazon Q3 2026 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000040/amzn-20260930.htm). Unlike the other cases, the comparison here is **nine months apart**, not two full year-ends.

Property and equipment increased by approximately **$151.8 billion**. That is huge capital expansion, consistent with investment in infrastructure. Long-term debt also rose sharply, reminding us that investments can be financed using borrowing and other forms of capital—not solely sales.

Notice accounts payable of **$158.72 billion** against inventory of **$42.63 billion**. Some suppliers are paid after the company has already sold goods or received customer money, which can support working capital. But Amazon's payable balance is not solely for its merchandise inventory, so those two figures should **not** be treated as a precise inventory-financing ratio.

Current assets were **$255.10 billion** versus current liabilities of **$257.98 billion** in September 2026: accounting working capital was **negative $2.88 billion**. Negative working capital is not automatically insolvency; it may reflect cash timing and the structure of the business. Liquidity also depends on cash equivalents, marketable securities, cash generation, borrowing access and when obligations mature.

## The cash flow statement: the decisive comparison

The following figures are **trailing twelve months (TTM) ended 30 September**, not simply Q3 figures.

| Cash metric, US$ billions | TTM Sep 2025 | TTM Sep 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Operating cash flow | 130.69 | 168.30 |
| Net cash property/equipment purchases used in Amazon's FCF reconciliation | 115.90 | 210.66 |
| **Free cash flow, Amazon definition** | **+14.79** | **−42.36** |

Source: [Amazon Q3 2026 earnings release, free-cash-flow reconciliation](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/).

Operating cash flow grew about **28.8%**. But cash investment in property and equipment, net of the specified proceeds and incentives, grew about **81.8%**.

The math explains the headline: **$168.30B − $210.66B = −$42.36B**. There is no contradiction. Amazon's operations generated more cash than before; the company spent *even more* on infrastructure, particularly to support AI and cloud capacity.

That is **negative free cash flow**, not negative operating cash flow. It is a sign of investment intensity and financing needs, but whether it creates value depends on the future cash those assets earn.

## Connecting the three statements: a server purchase

Imagine AWS buys **$10 billion of servers for cash**, which will be used over five years. This is a simplified example with straight-line depreciation, no residual value, and no financing or tax complications.

1. **Immediately:** cash falls $10 billion, servers appear as an asset, and investing cash flow decreases by $10 billion.
2. **Over time:** accounting depreciation of roughly **$2 billion per year** reduces operating profit. The original $10 billion is not expensed immediately through the income statement.
3. **In future operations:** customers pay for compute. Only if those expected future cash inflows and returns justify the purchase does the investment create economic value.

So earnings, assets and cash tell different chapters of the *same* story.

## What to watch next

Watch **AWS growth and margins, advertising profitability, infrastructure utilisation, future depreciation, net capital expenditure, operating cash flow and the trend in debt**. The essential question is whether newly installed capacity will earn attractive returns, not simply whether spending is high.

**In one sentence:** Amazon demonstrates why a company can have a thriving operating business and sharply negative free cash flow at the same time—and why the real test of capital allocation arrives in later years.
