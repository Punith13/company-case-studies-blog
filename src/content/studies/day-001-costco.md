Costco looks like a giant supermarket. Financially, it is something more interesting: **a low-margin retailer with a recurring membership business attached**. Understanding the combination explains why customers, suppliers and investors all see something different when they look at the same warehouse.

> **The big idea:** Costco does not need huge markups on every product. Memberships, shopping frequency and the speed at which merchandise is sold make the model work.

## First, follow one customer's money

Imagine paying an annual fee before entering a warehouse. Costco receives the money now and provides access over the following year. Then you buy groceries, appliances or petrol. Those purchases become merchandise sales.

The two revenue streams behave differently. A basket of groceries is a one-off sale; a membership is a payment for ongoing access, renewed by many customers each year. Costco reported a **worldwide membership renewal rate of 89.8%** at the end of FY2026. That is evidence of customer retention, though it does not guarantee future renewals.

## The income statement: thin margins, enormous volume

All amounts below are **US$ billions**, rounded, for fiscal years ending in August.

| Income statement | FY2025 | FY2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Merchandise net sales | 269.91 | 297.25 |
| Membership fees | 5.32 | 5.91 |
| **Total revenue** | **275.24** | **303.15** |
| Operating income | 10.38 | 11.69 |
| Net income | 8.10 | 9.23 |

Source: [Costco FY2026 Form 10-K, consolidated income statements](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm).

Revenue grew roughly **10.1%**; net income grew about **13.9%**. That is encouraging because profit increased faster than revenue. But notice the scale: operating income was only **3.9 cents for every dollar of total revenue** (11.685 ÷ 303.154).

Membership revenue was only **1.9% of total revenue**, yet its $5.91 billion size was equivalent to about **51% of operating income**. That comparison makes the membership business look important, but it is *not* correct to say all membership fees are pure profit: Costco still has costs of servicing members, and costs are not split out neatly that way.

The retailer's **merchandise gross margin** was about **11.1% of merchandise sales**. So when Costco sells $100 of merchandise, roughly $88.90 covers the merchandise cost, and around $11.10 remains *before* staff, buildings, administration and other expenses. Small differences in cost, customer traffic or pricing can matter a lot at this scale.

## The balance sheet: customers and suppliers help fund the machine

A balance sheet is a snapshot of what the company has and what it owes. These figures are **US$ billions at the year-end dates**.

| Balance sheet | Aug 2025 | Aug 2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash and cash equivalents | 14.16 | 20.21 |
| Merchandise inventory | 18.12 | 19.32 |
| Accounts payable to suppliers | 19.78 | 22.59 |
| Deferred membership fees | 2.85 | 3.01 |

Two relationships stand out.

**First, supplier credit.** At the end of FY2026, accounts payable of $22.59 billion exceeded recorded merchandise inventory of $19.32 billion by about **$3.27 billion**. Costco receives merchandise before paying all its suppliers. That helps financing, although a year-end comparison alone cannot tell us its precise average payment terms or whether all payables relate directly to that inventory.

**Second, deferred revenue.** Costco had **$3.01 billion of deferred membership fees**. That is not a mystery debt. It largely reflects access already paid for but not yet delivered. When someone buys a hypothetical $120 twelve-month membership on the final day of the year, cash increases immediately by $120. But only the service already provided is revenue; approximately $10 is recognised monthly over the next twelve months. Cash and a liability can rise together.

This is why **cash received** and **income earned** are different accounting concepts.

## The cash flow statement: are profits turning into spendable cash?

| Cash movement, US$ billions | FY2025 | FY2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Cash from operating activities | 13.34 | 15.82 |
| Cash spent on property and equipment | 5.50 | 6.44 |
| **Operating cash less that investment** | **7.84** | **9.38** |

Here, we calculate a simple **free-cash-flow proxy**: operating cash flow minus cash additions to property and equipment. This is money remaining *after* an important class of investment spending, not necessarily money that can all be distributed. Working capital, debt maturities and other commitments still matter.

Costco's FY2026 operating cash flow was **$15.82 billion**, well above its **$9.23 billion net income**. Depreciation is a non-cash expense; cash is also affected by the timing of inventory purchases, supplier payments and other liabilities. In FY2026, higher supplier payables helped operating cash flow, while increasing inventories used cash.

## Connect the three statements

A member pays upfront. The balance sheet records cash and an obligation; the income statement recognises revenue over the membership period; and operating cash flow reflects the money collected when it arrives.

Meanwhile, a new warehouse can require cash today, appear as an asset on the balance sheet, and affect earnings gradually through depreciation as it is used. Profits and cash therefore move on **different clocks**.

There is one more distinction: if Costco shares trade between two investors for $1,000 each, that money goes between those investors—not into Costco's warehouse bank account. Market valuation is a separate question from operational cash.

## What to watch next

The health of this model depends on **member renewals, comparable-store sales, gross margins, inventory turnover and the costs of opening new warehouses**. A rising share price does not prove all those indicators are improving, and a falling price does not prove the business is weakening.

**In one sentence:** Costco makes thin product margins attractive by combining high sales volume, customer loyalty, membership income and efficient cash timing.
