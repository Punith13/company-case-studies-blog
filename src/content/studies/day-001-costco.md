## 1 · How the company earns money — 3 minutes

Costco operates membership warehouses. Members pay an annual fee for access to warehouses selling food, household goods and other merchandise. Its economic engine combines **recurring membership fees** with rapid, high-volume merchandise turnover.

Open the [FY2026 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983226000093/cost-20260830.htm) for the year ended **30 August 2026**. Read the *Business* section and **Item 8 — Financial Statements**.

**Calculate:** Membership fees divided by total revenue. Then compare membership fees with operating income, recognising that fees are not entirely profit because memberships incur costs.

**Think:** Why can a relatively small revenue stream be so strategically important?

## 3 · Balance sheet — 5 minutes

Find *cash and cash equivalents*, *merchandise inventories*, *accounts payable* and *deferred membership fees* in the consolidated balance sheet.

**Calculate:** Accounts payable / merchandise inventories. What does that suggest about how inventory is financed? Why are membership fees a liability even when the company already holds the cash?

## 4 · Cash flow statement — 5 minutes

Find *net cash provided by operating activities* and *additions to property and equipment* for both years.

**Calculate:** Operating cash flow less additions to property and equipment as a simple proxy for free cash flow. Compare this with net income. Are profits and cash moving together?

## 5 · Connect the statements — 2 minutes

A member renews an annual membership on the final day of the financial year and pays immediately. What changes on that day in **cash**, **reported revenue** and **deferred membership fees**? What changes over the following twelve months?

> **Key concept:** Cash can arrive before revenue is recognised. Follow the timing across the statements.
